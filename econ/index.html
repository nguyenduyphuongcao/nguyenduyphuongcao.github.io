LSESU Economics essay competition · 2026 82 / 100, provisional

Collateral, Not Surveillance: The Home Day as a Worker Discipline Device

Written to a prompt set by Prof. Sir Christopher Pissarides. Firms treat the work-from-home day as something to be monitored. I argue the opposite: because workers value the day more than it costs the firm to provide, the day is the cheapest disciplinary instrument the firm owns, and surveillance spends down the very asset that makes it work.

The essay builds on Shapiro and Stiglitz on efficiency wages, Holmstrom and Milgrom on multitasking, and Falk and Kosfeld on control aversion, then sets out four design rules: ration and coordinate the day, contract on coarse outcomes, let peers monitor, and screen by self-selection.

Independent research · 2026

Pricing Without Scale: Revenue Management Adoption and Foregone Yield among Independent Hospitality Enterprises in Emerging Markets

Chains move their room rates daily. Independent firms in Vietnam hold theirs flat for a whole season. I integrated microeconomic theory, Littlewood's two-class rule for capacity constraints, with behavioral economics, dual entitlement and price fairness, to build a conceptual model evaluating an illustrative 11.7% foregone yield under static pricing, then asked why the gap survives.

The answer was not technology. A chain that raises its rates looks like it is following policy. An independent doing the same looks like it is taking advantage. The paper closes with a revenue optimisation framework incorporating behavioral economics metrics to maximise corporate yield functions under market constraints, built for firms too small to run a full pricing system.
